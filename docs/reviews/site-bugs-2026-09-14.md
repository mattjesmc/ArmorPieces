# The site after the split: five bugs, one cause

*2026-09-14. The user's list, verbatim, and what each turned out to be.*

> - Website still has the old "Ships with the mod" pack. Replace it all with the current packs we
>   have that are reviewed. The rest will come soon.
> - A lot of pieces have no picture yet
> - Some of the wardrobe sets have the wrong picture and showing only a piece.
> - Same for my personal sets.
> - Blockbench is not always working in the wardrobe.

## What was found

All five have one cause. The site's official content was the **0.3.0 release archive** (one
library entry, `armorpieces`, mirrored from the GitHub release: 91 pieces under `armorpieces:`),
while the editor it renders with is built from the mod's **main**, where the split moved every
one of those pieces into a pack with a namespace of its own (`armorpieces_knightly:great_helm`,
`armorpieces_hunt:antlers`, ...). Nothing that draws a picture could find what the site named:

- **No picture** (bullet 2): the 25 pieces the first split moved (`aerials`, `antlers`, `pelt`,
  `spine_ridge`, ...) had no thumbnail on the live site because `thumbnails.mjs` opened the mod's
  own entry from the bundle's example pack by id, and those ids were no longer in it. The two
  cloths never had one (no workspace to photograph them in).
- **Wrong picture, one piece** (bullets 3 and 4): `/wardrobe/<hash>.png` answered 503 for every
  set naming a moved piece - Chitin, Deep Tide, the Wild Hunt among the shipped ones, and every
  personal set that wore one - because `bb_rig.py --wear` in the editor found no geometry for
  `armorpieces:spine_ridge` (the box's app log had it: *"no geometry at
  assets/armorpieces/armorpieces/decoration/spine_ridge.json in /repo/src/main/resources"*, on
  every render attempt). The card then falls back to the stacked per-piece thumbnails behind the
  picture, and with most of those missing too, what showed was whichever single thumbnail
  existed: "only a piece".
- **Blockbench not always working** (bullet 5): the same `wear()` refusal, in the wardrobe page's
  live figure, for the same sets. (The `[editor] ... blckbn.ch` CSP lines in the log are
  Blockbench's own telemetry being blocked, and harmless.)
- **The old pack** (bullet 1): the archive itself, and `dist/site.json` in the mod's clone was
  stale on top - rebuilt before StageCommand moved to the built-in packs, so even the mod's own
  three sets named `armorpieces:banner`.

Two more things came out while fixing it, both real bugs of their own:

- The web editor's texture shim (`src/shim/25-textures.js` in ArmorPiecesBlockbench) only read
  sheets from under `/repo` and `/tmp`, so a piece opened from an **installed pack under
  `/packs/`** came up with placeholder sheets - every library thumbnail of a non-bundled pack was
  a flat yellow silhouette (the live site's `u-animal-pack` thumbnails show it). Checkouts opened
  in the browser had the same problem.
- The packs' `armorpieces-sets.json` files still dressed the split's old ids (`armorpieces:sash`
  where the Java says `armorpieces_court:sash`) and the Animals one was the pre-re-cut Menagerie;
  `stage_sets_to_packs.py` kept a declared set as-is and dropped every metal fitting (it read the
  trim-material tags under `minecraft:`, where there are none).

## What was done

**Mod (clone, pushed as part of this):** `dist/` rebuilt with `modpage build -t all` (the sets,
skins, fittings and loot groups wear their namespaces); `studio_figure.py` finds the plate skin
where the split put it (make_bundle's bake refused without it); `stage_sets_to_packs.py` refreshes
what a declared set wears from the Java and reads a fitting's tag from its own namespace; the
eight packs' sets files rewritten by it.

**Editor (ArmorPiecesBlockbench):** a library entry may be *sourced* from a folder of the mod
checkout instead of hosted zips (`"source": "packs/coral"`; the build zips it deterministically
with `build/zip.mjs` and records `build`, the hash of the zips). Nine official entries replace
the archive: `armorpieces` (the engine: fittings and cloths), `armorpieces-knightly`, `-court`,
`-wayfarer` (what the jar carries, `archive: true`), and `-coral`, `-animals`, `-hive`,
`-wildhunt`, `-dragonslayer` (installable, `requires 0.4.0`). The other six packs are added the
same way when they are reviewed: one JSON file each under `library/entries/`. `thumbnails.mjs`
photographs every entry in one browser boot, each installed as its own pack. The bundle carries
the three built-in packs under `packs/` the way the jar does (so the example set dresses and
`bb_rig` finds the mod's own pieces), and the texture shim reads sheets from `/packs/` too.

**Site (branch `packs-on-the-site`):** the seed reads every official entry, marks each by its
`build`, and records the hashes it read; when an entry changes it **retires** official objects
no current entry carries (official off, unpublished - hidden, kept) and rewrites every saved set
that names a retired id through the new objects' `former_ids` (`followMoves`), so personal
outfits move with the pieces the way a save does in game. Thumbnails of the mod's own pieces are
looked up through the index (`officialPiece`), not under a hardcoded `armorpieces/`. The wardrobe
pack handed to the live figure and to the picture renderer now carries the mod's own pieces from
the packs the bundle does not have (`includeOfficial`; the jar's three are left out); every
"is this a variant of the mod's piece" heuristic goes through `officialNamespaces()`; a composed
pack's *requires* line names the packs a borrowed piece needs.

**Deploy:** CI needs nothing new - the mod is checked out beside the editor, and
`make_library.mjs` defaults to `../ArmorPieces`. On the box the seed runs once at first need after
the deploy: the 0.3.0 rows retire, the sets follow, and the pictures re-render under the new
editor stamp.
